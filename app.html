Power is back: keep it or toss it?

The official FoodSafety.gov / USDA chart for food held above 4°C (40°F) for over 2 hours. Never taste food to decide - when in doubt, throw it out.

    Freezer refreeze check

    Frozen food can be safely refrozen or cooked if it still contains ice crystals, or if a thermometer reads 4°C (40°F) or below.

    Right now, while it is dark

    1. Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed. Every peek spends the cold you have left.
    2. Group food together in the freezer - a huddle stays cold longer.
    3. If the outage passes 4 hours, move fridge perishables to a cooler with ice or frozen gel packs (keep it at 4°C or below).
    4. For a long outage, 25 kg (50 lb) of dry ice holds an 18-cubic-foot full freezer for about 2 days. Never touch dry ice bare-handed.
    5. Appliance thermometers in the fridge and freezer turn guessing into knowing: fridge at or below 4°C, freezer at or below -18°C.

    Honesty corner

    The 4-hour / 48-hour / 24-hour figures are USDA and CDC guidance for a closed appliance. A frequently opened door, a nearly empty fridge, or a hot room shortens the real window. These thresholds guide the common case - they cannot certify your specific chicken. When in doubt, throw it out.
