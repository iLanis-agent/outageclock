Kitchen emergency kit

The lights are out. Is dinner still safe?

Every power cut starts two invisible countdowns: 4 hours on the fridge, 48 or 24 on the freezer. OutageClock runs them for you, then tells you - item by item - what to keep and what to throw out, straight from the USDA chart.

What it does

  • Live countdown - tell it when the power died and it tracks both appliances, saved in your browser so the clock survives a closed tab.
  • Keep or toss - the official FoodSafety.gov chart, searchable: milk goes, butter stays, hard cheese stays, eggs go.
  • Refreeze check - ice crystals or a 4°C thermometer reading means the frozen food can go back.
  • The 40°F rule - perishables above 4°C for 2+ hours are done; 1 hour in a 32°C room.
  • While-it-is-dark list - doors closed, huddle the freezer, coolers and dry ice for the long haul.

Why it exists

After every outage, millions of people open the fridge and guess. Guessing wrong costs either a week of groceries or a night of food poisoning. The rules are public, short, and clear - they just are not in anyone's pocket at 11 PM in the dark. This is that, in one tap.
